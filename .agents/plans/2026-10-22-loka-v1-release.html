What the decisions cost
  • Unsigned. Windows SmartScreen warns on every download until enough people run a given build; the release notes say so.
  • Helium’s crash server and services. Helium’s privacy policy covers Helium, not forks; Loka’s policy names the services and points to theirs. Helium honors --custom-crash-server-url, so moving crash reports later needs no patch.
  • Version. WinSparkle compares the exe’s version with the feed’s, so the exe, feed, zip and tag must share one number. Loka bumps revision.txt per release; a rebuild then rewrites chrome/VERSION.
What I re-checked from the audit, and what I found on the way
  • The bookmarks page’s Google promo and the extensions page’s “managed by your administrator” aren’t on screen; the audit read hidden text. Screenshots in .agents/verify-runs/20261022-195140-browser/.

  • Keys typed in the first second after Ctrl+N in a new window are lost: the command bar’s page is still loading. A pre-loaded bar would fix it; small, after v1.
  • Two flaky browser checks were timing races in the tests, now fixed: Escape in a new window’s bar (#37) and the QA extension’s sleeping worker (#40).
